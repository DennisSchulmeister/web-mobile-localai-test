Internet of Things
==================

![Deeply Embedded, Embedded, Internet of Things](embedded-iot.png)

IoT devices are higher-performance embedded systems with an almost permanent
Internet connection. The original definition from 1999 envisaged the
machine-readable identification of physical objects using RFID tags. Today,
the term refers to electronic devices with embedded, Internet-connected
computer modules that can be uniquely addressed worldwide via an IP address.
In a broader sense, the Internet of Things also includes infrastructure,
cloud platforms, and backend services through which devices can be connected
to one another, managed, controlled, and monitored.

The Internet of Things is an umbrella term for a global IT infrastructure
that connects physical and virtual objects with the aim of enabling them to
work together. The idea emerged around 1991 in an essay by Mark Weiser under
the term “Ubiquitous Computing”. This encompasses ubiquitous objects equipped
with sensors that are seamlessly integrated into their surroundings and are
therefore no longer directly perceived as computer systems. Additional
actuators enable actions to be performed as well as states to be detected.

The original implementation began around the turn of the millennium with
RFID tags attached to objects that could be read without contact. However,
this can only be regarded as a precursor to the Internet of Things, since it
lacks a means for objects to communicate directly via Internet protocols.
Today, the Internet of Things encompasses a wide range of technologies,
services, and business models. These are based on:

* The standardization of components and services in the Internet of Things

* The introduction of easily accessible, secure, and universal network
  connectivity

* The reduction of costs for participants integrated into the IoT

* The development of low-cost, automated digital services within the network
  as a benefit of connectivity.
